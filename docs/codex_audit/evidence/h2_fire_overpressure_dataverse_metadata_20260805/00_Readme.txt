Description of dataset: 
This dataset includes results from laboratory-scale experiments examining the behavior of ignited hydrogen jets in open atmospheric conditions with delay ignition. The tests investigate flame acceleration, overpressure development, and the influence of obstacle configurations on flame propagation. Key parameters varied during the experiments include ignition location, mass flow rates, and the spacing between obstacles and the nozzle. Using high-speed imaging and pressure sensors, the dataset provides detailed  data on combustion dynamics.

METHODOLOGICAL INFORMATION
--------------------------
Description of sources and methods used for collection/generation of data:
The experiments were conducted using a laboratory‑scale hydrogen release and ignition rig designed to investigate delayed ignition of under‑expanded hydrogen jets and the resulting flame acceleration and overpressure development. 
Hydrogen was released through a circular nozzle into open atmospheric conditions, with ignition triggered after a controlled delay. A range of configurations was tested, varying hydrogen mass flow rate, ignition position, and spacing between the nozzle and obstacles. High‑speed imaging and pressure measurements were used to characterize the combustion dynamics and overpressure development.
Four piezoelectric pressure sensors were mounted along the rig to capture transient overpressure during ignition and flame propagation. These sensors were chosen for their high frequency response, enabling accurate detection of the rapid pressure rise associated with hydrogen deflagrations. The sensors were connected to a high‑speed data acquisition (DAQ) system with sampling rates sufficient to resolve microsecond‑scale pressure changes.

Methods for processing the data:
The piezoelectric transducers used for the overpressure measurements employed a linear calibration curve of:
1 V=−1 bar
The upstream pressure in the hydrogen feed line was measured using a separate pressure transmitter with a different calibration characteristic than the piezoelectric sensors. The transducer provided a linear voltage output that was converted to physical pressure using:
p=(V−V0)⋅62.5p
where:
V is the instantaneous measured voltage,
V_0 is the zero‑offset voltage corresponding to atmospheric pressure at ambient conditions,
62.5 is the calibration slope in units of bar/V (or equivalently, the gain needed to map the voltage signal to absolute upstream pressure).
